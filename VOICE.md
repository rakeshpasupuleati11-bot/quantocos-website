# VOICE.md — how GC³ writes

Plain English. Short sentences. Concrete over abstract. If a sentence would survive
being cut, it does not belong on the page.

## Four words, defined once each

- **Pod** — a monthly team you subscribe to: one named manager, human specialists and a
  set of AI agents, working inside your tools.
- **Credit** — the unit the portal bills in. $25 each, one rate for everyone. Every brief
  is estimated in credits before work starts.
- **Gate** — one of five approvals every deliverable passes: G1 brief approval,
  G2 direction selection, G3 internal QA, G4 client review, G5 go-live.
- **Guild** — the group of practitioners who hold the craft standard for a track and
  review work across pods.

Also define on first use: **brand ingestion** (loading your brand assets, voice and
templates into the pod at onboarding) and **Pilot Sprint** (a 14-day, 60-credit start).

## Rules

1. Say the number, or say nothing. No "significant", "substantial", "leading".
2. Two sentences per paragraph. Three only in a table cell or a legal line.
3. Name the owner of every claim: "your manager", "the portal", "the engagement letter".
4. Never promise what the site does not: no delivery-time promise, no ROI figure,
   no client outcomes we cannot show.
5. H2s say what the reader gets, not what we feel. "What you can check before you sign",
   not "Our commitment to excellence".

## Banned words and phrases

`unlimited` · `guaranteed` / `guarantee` · `ROI` · `replaces` (as a claim about a role
or team) · `within 24 hours` / `within 48 hours` (except the G4 review window) ·
`best-in-class` · `world-class` · `industry-leading` · `five fixed-price` ·
any invented number (client counts, outcomes, benchmarks, competitor prices).

Use instead: the actual term, the actual number, or nothing.

### Two exemptions the checker honours

1. `no ROI figures` — allowed only inside the sentence that explicitly disclaims
   ROI ("no turnaround-hour promises, no ROI figures, no invented benchmarks").
2. `within 24 hours` — allowed only as a contractual incident-notification
   commitment (security.html), never as a delivery promise.
