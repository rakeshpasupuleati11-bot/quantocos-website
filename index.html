Why the month gets hard

Your business shouldn'trun on your follow-ups.

A growing business needs a steady stream of design, content and campaigns. Four gaps make that expensive to sustain.

  • Retainers buy hours, not output. The invoice rarely maps to what shipped.
  • Hiring takes a quarter you do not have. By the time someone joins, the campaign has moved.
  • Freelancers do not share a standard. Someone internal has to become the editor.
  • The invoice is the first cost you see. There is no unit price to plan against.

What do I actually get each month?

A pod, a named manager and a credit pool sized to the pod. The plan is agreed at Gate 1 and reported against each month.

Deliverables are listed in the brief, so scope is not left to interpretation.

How do credits work?

One rate of $25 applies across pods. Each deliverable uses credits at a published rate, and the calculator gives an estimate before you commit.

If the estimate is too high, you can cut items at the brief stage rather than discover the cost on the invoice.

Can I change pods or cancel?

Yes. Terms are month to month, with written notice before the next billing date. There is no setup fee and no exit fee.

You can move up a pod when volume grows; the change is confirmed in writing.

Who does the work?

Named specialists working from India, overlapping your business day, with independent delivery partners in some markets.

A pod manager owns quality, communication and the weekly update.

Is there a minimum term?

No lock-in. Start with the 14-day Pilot Sprint at $1,500 for 60 credits, then move to a pod if it fits.

The pilot uses the same gates and the same credit rate as a monthly pod.
