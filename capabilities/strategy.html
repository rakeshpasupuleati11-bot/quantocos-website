The friction

Why this work stalls in a normal supplier model.

01

The deck does not survive Monday

A strategy is presented, admired and then disconnected from the workstreams that must carry it.

02

Positioning is asserted, not tested

Teams pick a phrase in a workshop without checking whether the buyer uses the same language.

03

Nobody owns the trade-offs

Every option is kept open, so the plan has no priority order and no decision record.

What the pod actually makes.

How the month runs

From brief to release, with a gate between each step.

The pod does not skip a gate because a deadline moved. It re-scopes at the next gate instead.

Brief

The property, audience, channels and deadline are written into one scope.

Direction

The pod agrees the treatment and the production route at Gate 2.

Production

Specialists build the items; internal QA checks them at Gate 3.

Review and release

You review at Gate 4, then Gate 5 releases the files and sources.

Where it fits

A leadership team that has to make a decision and defend it.

The Strategy Pod fits before a launch, a repositioning or a portfolio choice, when the cost of an unclear decision is higher than the cost of the pod.

  • A market entry or repositioning
  • A portfolio or pricing decision
  • A story that sales and marketing must share

How is a strategy engagement scoped?

The question, the decision date and the output are agreed first. The pod then sizes the work in credits rather than selling an open-ended retainer.

Do you run the workshops in person?

Workshops run remotely by default, with in-person sessions scoped separately. The deliverable is the decision record, not the session itself.

What if the answer is to do nothing?

That is a valid output. A decision memo can recommend stopping, and the pod will say so in writing.
